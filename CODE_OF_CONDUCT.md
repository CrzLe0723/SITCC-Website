# SITCC Code of Conduct

**Version 2.0**

The St. Ignatius Coding Club (SITCC) is committed to providing a safe, welcoming, respectful, and collaborative environment where students can learn, create, experiment, and grow through technology.

This Code of Conduct explains the standards we expect from everyone participating in SITCC and how concerns about behavior may be handled.

---

## 1. Our Pledge

We, as members, contributors, officers, mentors, and leaders of SITCC, pledge to make participation in our community a respectful and welcoming experience for everyone.

SITCC is a place to:

* Learn from one another
* Ask questions without fear of being mocked
* Share ideas and projects
* Give and receive constructive feedback
* Collaborate on games, software, and other projects
* Make mistakes and learn from them
* Help other members grow as programmers and creators

We recognize that SITCC members may have different backgrounds, experiences, opinions, skill levels, interests, and ways of learning.

We therefore pledge to treat others with **respect, kindness, patience, and empathy**, and to contribute to an environment where everyone can participate safely.

---

# 2. Our Standards

Examples of behavior that contribute to a positive SITCC community include:

* Treating other members, officers, mentors, teachers, and community members with respect.
* Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward others.
* Listening to different viewpoints and experiences.
* Giving constructive feedback rather than insulting or attacking someone.
* Accepting constructive criticism gracefully.
* Helping new or less-experienced members learn.
* Giving appropriate credit to people whose work, ideas, or resources you use.
* Respecting other people's projects, code, artwork, music, and other creative work.
* Taking responsibility for mistakes and making a reasonable effort to correct them.
* Asking for help when you need it.
* Respecting personal boundaries.
* Following reasonable instructions from SITCC officers, mentors, teachers, and other authorized leaders.
* Following the **SITCC Rulelist** and applicable school rules.
* Using SITCC's technology, repositories, communication platforms, and other resources responsibly.
* Focusing on what is best for the SITCC community rather than only individual interests.
* Helping make SITCC a place where people feel comfortable learning and creating.

Being a beginner is never a reason to be treated poorly. **Everyone was a beginner once.**

# 3. Unacceptable Behavior

The following behaviors are not acceptable within SITCC.

### Harassment and Discrimination

* Harassing, intimidating, threatening, or bullying another person.
* Targeting someone because of their identity, background, abilities, beliefs, experience level, or other personal characteristics.
* Repeatedly making someone uncomfortable after they have asked you to stop.
* Sexual harassment, sexualized language, sexual attention, or sexual advances.
* Sharing sexualized or otherwise inappropriate content in SITCC spaces.

### Personal Attacks

* Insults, humiliation, or deliberately degrading comments directed at another person.
* Personal attacks during disagreements.
* Mocking someone's programming ability, project, appearance, intelligence, or experience.
* Deliberately excluding or discouraging someone from participating.

Disagreement is allowed. **Disrespect is not.**

### Disruptive Behavior

* Trolling or intentionally disrupting SITCC activities.
* Repeatedly derailing discussions or meetings.
* Intentionally interfering with another person's work.
* Spamming SITCC communication channels, repositories, issues, or other community spaces.
* Deliberately creating unnecessary conflicts or drama.

### Privacy and Personal Information

* Publishing or distributing another person's private information without their permission.
* Sharing private messages, screenshots, photographs, recordings, or other personal information inappropriately.
* Attempting to obtain another person's passwords, account credentials, or other private information.
* Using another person's account or identity without permission.

### Technology and Projects

* Intentionally damaging, deleting, or sabotaging another person's work.
* Intentionally introducing malicious code or other harmful content into SITCC projects.
* Misusing SITCC accounts, repositories, websites, devices, or other technology.
* Taking credit for another person's work.
* Plagiarizing code, artwork, music, writing, or other creative work.
* Ignoring applicable software licenses or attribution requirements.

### Other Conduct

* Threats of violence or other serious threats.
* Encouraging dangerous or harmful behavior.
* Retaliating against someone for making a good-faith report.
* Knowingly making false reports intended to harm another person.
* Repeatedly violating this Code of Conduct or the SITCC Rulelist.
* Any other behavior that reasonably creates an unsafe, hostile, or seriously disruptive environment.

This list is not exhaustive. SITCC leadership may address behavior that is not specifically listed when it conflicts with the purpose or safety of the community.

# 4. SITCC Spaces

This Code of Conduct applies to official SITCC activities and community spaces, including:

* SITCC meetings and events
* SITCC projects
* Official SITCC websites
* SITCC GitHub repositories and organizations
* GitHub issues, discussions, pull requests, and comments associated with SITCC
* Official SITCC communication channels
* Online collaboration spaces used for SITCC activities
* SITCC social media accounts and other official accounts
* School activities where someone is participating as a representative of SITCC

This Code of Conduct may also apply to behavior outside official SITCC spaces when that behavior directly affects the safety, participation, or ability of members to take part in SITCC.

When SITCC activities take place on school property or as part of a school activity, **applicable St. Ignatius and school policies also apply**.

---

# 5. Leadership Responsibilities

SITCC officers and other authorized leaders are responsible for helping maintain a safe and productive community.

Leaders should:

* Model the behavior expected from members.
* Take concerns seriously.
* Listen to people involved in an incident fairly.
* Avoid unnecessary public discussion of private reports.
* Take reasonable action when this Code of Conduct is violated.
* Explain moderation decisions when appropriate.
* Seek help from an appropriate adult, teacher, school administrator, parent/guardian, or other responsible authority when a situation requires it.

SITCC leaders are not expected to handle serious safety concerns alone.

When a situation involves potential danger, threats, serious harassment, abuse, or another matter requiring adult or school intervention, SITCC leadership may escalate the matter to the appropriate responsible adult or authority.

# 7. Handling Reports

SITCC will make a reasonable effort to review reports promptly, fairly, and respectfully.

Depending on the situation, leaders may:

1. Speak privately with the person who reported the concern.
2. Speak with other people involved.
3. Review relevant messages, commits, issues, or other information.
4. Give the person involved an opportunity to explain their actions.
5. Determine whether the Code of Conduct or another SITCC rule was violated.
6. Take an appropriate response.
7. Involve an appropriate adult or school authority when necessary.

Reports will be handled with discretion. However, **confidentiality cannot be guaranteed in situations where disclosure is necessary for someone's safety or is required by school policy or another applicable rule.**

SITCC will not publicly share unnecessary personal information about people involved in a report.

# 8. Protection From Retaliation

Retaliation against someone who makes a good-faith report or participates in an investigation is not permitted.

Examples of retaliation include:

* Threatening someone because they made a report.
* Harassing someone for participating in an investigation.
* Attempting to punish or exclude someone for making a legitimate report.
* Encouraging others to target someone because they reported a concern.

Concerns about retaliation may themselves be addressed as violations of this Code of Conduct.

---

# 9. Possible Responses

Not every violation requires the same response.

SITCC leadership will consider factors such as:

* The seriousness of the behavior
* Whether anyone was harmed or placed at risk
* Whether the behavior was intentional
* Whether the behavior has happened before
* Whether the person takes responsibility
* Whether the person cooperates with efforts to resolve the situation
* The effect of the behavior on the SITCC community

Possible responses include the following.

## Level 1 — Reminder & Correction

**Community impact:**
A minor or first-time violation that does not create a serious safety concern.

**Possible response:**
A private conversation or written reminder explaining the issue and the expected behavior.

The member may be asked to correct the behavior, apologize, or take another reasonable step to resolve the situation.

---

## Level 2 — Formal Warning

**Community impact:**
A more significant violation or repeated behavior after a previous reminder.

**Possible response:**
A formal warning from SITCC leadership.

Additional conditions may be placed on participation when appropriate.

Continued violations may result in restricted participation or removal from SITCC activities.

---

## Level 3 — Restricted Participation

**Community impact:**
A serious violation, repeated violations, or behavior that significantly disrupts the community.

**Possible response:**
Temporary restriction from some or all SITCC activities or community spaces.

This may include temporary restrictions on:

* Meetings
* Projects
* GitHub repositories
* Online communication spaces
* SITCC events
* Other official SITCC activities

When appropriate, an authorized adult, teacher, parent/guardian, or school administrator may be involved.

---

## Level 4 — Removal From SITCC

**Community impact:**
Severe misconduct, serious harassment, threats, intentional harm, repeated serious violations, or behavior that makes continued participation unsafe or impractical.

**Possible response:**
Removal from SITCC activities and/or official SITCC community spaces.

Where required or appropriate, the matter may also be referred to school administration or another responsible authority.

---

## Immediate Escalation

Certain situations may require immediate action without progressing through the levels above.

Examples include:

* Credible threats of violence
* Serious safety concerns
* Serious harassment or abuse
* Deliberate attempts to compromise accounts or systems
* Serious malicious behavior
* Conduct requiring immediate adult or school intervention

In these situations, SITCC leadership may immediately restrict participation and contact an appropriate adult, school official, parent/guardian, or other responsible authority.

---

# 10. Fairness and Proportionality

SITCC will attempt to respond to violations proportionally to their seriousness.

A first-time minor mistake should not automatically receive the same response as intentional serious misconduct.

At the same time, a person's skill level, popularity, position within SITCC, friendships, or role as an officer should not excuse harmful behavior.

**SITCC leadership should apply these standards consistently while considering the circumstances of each situation.**

---

# 11. Appeals and Reconsideration

If a member believes that a moderation decision was made unfairly or that important information was not considered, they may request that the decision be reconsidered.

Appeals should focus on relevant facts, new information, or concerns about whether the Code of Conduct was applied appropriately.

For serious matters involving school policies or student safety, the appropriate school authority may have final authority over the situation.

---

# 12. The SITCC Rulelist

This Code of Conduct works alongside the **SITCC Rulelist**.

The Rulelist contains additional expectations for participation in SITCC, while this Code of Conduct focuses primarily on respectful behavior, community safety, and handling misconduct.

Members are expected to follow both.

If the two documents appear to conflict, SITCC leadership should interpret them in a way that best protects member safety, respects applicable school policies, and supports the purpose of SITCC.

---

# 13. A Community Built to Learn

SITCC exists so students can learn about programming, game development, technology, creativity, and collaboration.

Making mistakes is part of learning.

Asking a beginner question is part of learning.

Getting a bug wrong is part of learning.

Disagreeing about how something should be programmed is part of collaboration.

What matters is how we treat one another while doing those things.

**Be curious. Be respectful. Help others grow. Build something great.**
